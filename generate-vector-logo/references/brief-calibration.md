# Brief calibration

## 1. Decompose the reference

| Layer | Question | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Structure | What silhouette, primitive rhythm, spacing, and negative space define the mark? | Extract abstract principles only. |
| System | What remains invariant across applications, and what changes? | Reuse invariant-versus-variable logic. |
| Surface | Which colors, materials, textures, lighting, or motion are applied? | Explore only after a flat original mark works. |

Do not confuse a memorable finish with the logo itself. Borrow the system idea, not the silhouette.

## 2. Establish originality distance

Adopt abstract traits such as compactness, modularity, corner character, density, and tone. Deliberately change signature module arrangements, exact proportions, posture, cutouts, and identifying palettes. Never adopt or generate alphabet-derived forms.

Change at least two governing properties: construction, primitive family, proportions, posture, negative-space concept, or corner character. For a famous or highly distinctive reference, change three.

## 3. Build a concept hierarchy

1. **Primary reading** — what the silhouette is immediately.
2. **Secondary reading** — the product idea discovered through structure or negative space.
3. **Expression layer** — optional colors, materials, animation, or mockups.

The secondary reading must modify the primary construction. Avoid attaching a miniature spreadsheet, sparkle, link, or badge to a generic subject. Reject initials, monograms, hidden letters, and typographic silhouettes even when they arise accidentally.

## 4. Write the working prompt

> Design an original **[mark type]** for **[product/category]**. The primary silhouette is **[subject]**. Construct it from **[primitive family and geometry]** so **[secondary meaning]** emerges through **[shared construction or negative space]**. The character is **[3–5 adjectives]**. The master must work in pure black at **[smallest size]**. Use **[reference]** only for **[abstract system traits]**; do not reproduce **[signature reference features]**. Create **[3–5]** directions that differ by **[construction strategies]**. Reject **[known failure modes]**. Explore **[surface treatments]** only after the flat silhouette is approved.

## 5. Run the concept gate

- Does the silhouette communicate the primary subject without color?
- Does the silhouette and its negative space avoid alphabetic or typographic readings?
- Is the concept independent of the brand's name, spelling, and initials?
- Is the secondary meaning structural rather than decorative?
- Can the mark be described without naming the reference?
- Would it remain distinct beside the reference?
- Are finishes optional rather than structural?
- Does it survive at 16, 24, and 32 px?

If any answer is no, revise the thesis or construction before polishing paths.
